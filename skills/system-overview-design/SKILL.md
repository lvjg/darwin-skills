# System Overview Design

## Purpose and Operating Model

Form the system-level judgment the user needs and express it as a coherent design artifact. Begin with what the reader must understand or decide and the observable result that matters. Explain the existing system only when it changes that judgment. When a new or changed result is required, establish the real problem, explore viable directions, choose the best-supported smallest complete design, close its necessary mechanisms, and define how the result can be accepted.

Keep evidence, authority, design, and implementation status distinct throughout the work. State existing behavior only within the boundary established by code, contracts, tests, runtime evidence, or accountable owners. Justify a proposed design from the requested outcome and governing constraints; do not use it to fill an unknown fact, treat the current implementation as the automatic target, or imply that an adopted design is implemented. These are claim disciplines, not separate workflows or document modes.

Choose `create`, `complete`, or `rewrite` only as the editing operation. For completion or rewrite, preserve still-valid intent, evidence, responsibilities, contracts, mechanisms, decisions, and behavior until an authorized change or stronger evidence establishes what remains, moves, changes, or disappears. Preserve an adopted route unless the user or accountable owner changes it or authorizes redesign against the established goals and constraints. A newly formed or materially revised route remains `Pending Confirmation` until adopted.

Use the five stages below as one revisable reasoning chain. The design model governs every stage. Read `references/overview-standard.md` only after the judgment has converged; it controls expression, not design formation. Do not turn the method, investigation, or candidate search into the document structure.

## Unified Design Model

### Governing Design Mind

Start from the observable result that a user, consumer, operator, or responsible party must be able to rely on. Establish the obligation that makes a result, guarantee, or constraint necessary before designing state, recovery, compatibility, migration, or another quality mechanism. Separate established intent, facts, constraints, inherited decisions, new decisions, assumptions, risks, and unresolved conditions, and limit every conclusion to the evidence and authority that support it.

Treat the existing system as relevant evidence and an evolution constraint, not as the boundary of the solution space. Look beyond the current repository when an active external contract, adjacent responsibility, maintained platform capability, established standard, or mature solution can materially change the judgment. Stop expanding the investigation when more context cannot change the problem, overall direction, responsibility boundary, key mechanism, acceptance, or major risk.

Prefer reuse, direct change, deletion, and clear responsibility before new structure. Seek the smallest complete design, not the fewest boxes or the richest architecture. Challenge the current implementation, the user's proposed means, and the preferred candidate against the strongest viable simpler or established route. When a premise changes, re-derive dependent conclusions and delete invalidated machinery instead of preserving it as a safer branch. Stop when deeper analysis can only perfect an unrequired mechanism or implementation detail.

### System Design Standard

Use these properties as causal tests for the design, not as document sections, slogans, or a scorecard. First establish the outcomes, preserved behavior, external commitments, authorized degradation, correctness conditions, and material constraints that actually govern the work. Reject any direction that violates them; compare tradeoffs only among valid directions. When the task explains an existing system, use the same properties only to expose gaps material to that explanation—do not turn them into an unsolicited redesign.

- **Outcome correctness:** Every mechanism must contribute to a stable result that an actual consumer or responsible party can observe and rely on. Carry established outcomes and preserved behavior through applicable success, failure, concurrency, and degradation. Do not substitute local technical completion for the business or operating result, or add a quality guarantee merely to make the design look complete.
- **Responsibility and decision clarity:** Assign every material fact, policy, decision, state change, side effect, and failure response to the boundary best able to guarantee its meaning and correctness. Separate decision authority from execution, representation maintenance, and recovery only when the handoff changes an outcome, rule, contract, or failure behavior. Use contracts to bind collaboration without duplicating business policy, creating competing decision sources, or leaving results ownerless.
- **Information and state integrity:** Distinguish original facts, derived results, durable state, caches, events, projections, and external representations by authority and dependency. Retain cross-operation state only when an established outcome, active contract, actual consumer, durable fact, committed effect, observed failure, or operating responsibility requires it. Once required, define its source or arbitration rule, writes and reads, validity, transitions, invalidation, conflict handling, reconciliation, recovery, and retirement. A subordinate representation must not silently acquire decision authority.
- **Boundaries and change containment:** Derive boundaries from business responsibility, decision authority, change drivers, contracts, trust, lifecycle, and failure scope rather than the package tree. Keep business policy independently changeable from transport, persistence, framework, vendor, deployment, and presentation when their owners or change drivers differ. Align dependency direction with responsibility and stability, expose necessary contracts, hide replaceable choices, and localize necessary coupling rather than maximizing layers or separation.
- **Justified structure:** Add a boundary, abstraction, protocol, state, configuration, cache, extension point, or compatibility path only when it centralizes a real invariant, serves an actual consumer, contains an evidenced change, or closes an established correctness, operating, or evolution gap that a simpler maintained route cannot close. Weigh the benefit against indirection, synchronization, learning, operation, migration, and removal cost. Similar syntax, possible reuse, or hypothetical growth is not enough.
- **Runtime and evolution closure:** Carry every established outcome through the applicable handoffs, partial or indeterminate completion, failure detection, takeover, observability, and evolution. Define recovery responsibility when an outcome and its record or handoff cannot be completed atomically. For a changing system, model only necessary coexistence, cutover, rollback, irreversible points, and old-path exit. Infrastructure readiness is not system completion.

### Decision Model

- **Keep decision focus:** Organize the work around the understanding or design outcome the user needs and the questions that can determine it. Investigate a secondary quality concern, external capability, risk, or future condition only as far as it can change the judgment, responsibility, material contract, key mechanism, acceptance, or major risk. Treat overdesign, extensibility, recovery, performance, and elegance as design lenses, not goals or mandatory findings. Simplicity breaks ties among complete valid directions; it does not remove complexity required by an established obligation or evidenced failure.
- **Use the relevant horizon:** Derive the design horizon from an active commitment, governing contract, necessary transition, or evidenced change within the system's intended life. A plausible future demand may shape present change containment when evidence makes it decision-relevant, but does not justify delivering future capability, a roadmap, phase plan, or compatibility path without an active commitment and accountable trigger. Otherwise preserve evolvability through clear boundaries and a reconsideration condition.
- **Select hierarchically:** First eliminate directions that cannot satisfy the established outcomes, preserved behavior, hard constraints, decision authority, or viable responsibility and state semantics. Then compare the remaining directions by ownership clarity, change containment, runtime correctness, lifecycle cost, reversibility, operating burden, and evidence strength. No score or attractive technical property compensates for a violated obligation.
- **Admit lifecycle-expanding mechanisms explicitly:** When a design adds decision authority, an authoritative source, persistent state or another representation, cross-boundary coordination, an independent operating owner, an externally owned contract change, compatibility or migration, an irreversible effect, a trust boundary, or material deployment and retirement cost, state the established obligation, the simplest viable maintained route and its evidenced gap, how the mechanism closes it, its owner and actual consumer, and its lifecycle cost. Ask what established result fails if the mechanism is removed; without an answer, remove it rather than completing its lifecycle.
- **Reset invalid premises:** Rebind the judgment to the latest user constraints and governing contracts whenever either changes. Reopen only dependent conclusions and delete derived state, recovery, compatibility, migration, and evolution as one chain. Revisions that keep generating version markers, legacy access, draining, reconciliation, or recovery state signal a direction problem: recheck the founding obligation and the simpler removal route before extending the machinery.

Interpret explicit constraints without silently narrowing them. In particular, treat `stateless` as the capability owning no persistent state for recovery or replay across operations. If an established external-effect obligation conflicts with that constraint, expose the conflict and obtain the user's choice before adding such state to the capability or a new collaborator.

Treat a proposed change to an externally owned wire schema, field meaning, version, or release sequence as a route-changing condition. Establish the contract, semantic owner, actual consumers, authority to request the change, and owner acceptance before treating the route as adopted or deriving migration, compatibility, cutover, persistence, or recovery from it. Until then, keep the condition visible and test the unchanged contract and the smallest system-owned mapping when its semantics are known.

## Stage 1: Frame the Required System Judgment

Define what the reader must understand or decide, the observable result in scope, behavior that must be preserved or explained, change that must be designed, allowed degradation, relevant participants and environments, scope and non-goals, material constraints, decision horizon, decision authority, and unresolved conditions that can change the judgment. Identify the primary question and the few concerns capable of changing its answer. Capacity, latency, security, privacy, compliance, reliability, and operability are material only when they change the result, responsibility, route, failure behavior, evolution, or acceptance.

Separate desired results, symptoms or starting conditions, causal claims, constraints, inherited decisions, and proposed means. A requested technology or mechanism is not automatically the goal. Test premises that can change scope, guarantee strength, or direction. Do not manufacture a redesign when explanation is sufficient, expand to system design when a local change closes the result, or invent a baseline for greenfield work.

For completion or rewrite, anchor the revision to the latest applicable intent: the judgment the artifact must support, outcomes and preserved behavior, material evidence, scope and non-goals, constraints and allowed degradation, adopted route, acceptance meaning, and the source of each consequential item. “Latest” means applicable under the governing authority and scope, not merely the newest text. Leave conflicting authoritative inputs unresolved until their owner decides. Use a lightweight working anchor only when several changes or omission risks justify it.

Classify a requested change before applying it: an **intent change** alters the required judgment, outcome, scope, constraint, or acceptance; an **evidence correction** changes what can be claimed about existing behavior; a **route change** alters an adopted design; a mechanism, structural, or editorial correction stays within the same intent and route. Review feedback, implementation detail, newly found evidence, or a preferred mechanism does not by itself authorize an intent or route change. When authority changes the intent, update the anchor and re-derive every dependent conclusion. When evidence changes, update only dependent claims. Delete superseded intent and machinery rather than retaining them as a precautionary branch.

Match sources to claims: users and accountable owners establish intent and adoption; contracts establish external commitments; runtime evidence establishes behavior in a specific environment; implementation establishes the present path; tests establish protected expectations. Keep assumptions, risks, and missing evidence or authority distinct. Proceed when the required judgment, result, scope, preservation obligations, authority, and judgment-changing unknowns can be stated independently of the module layout.

## Stage 2: Understand the Relevant System and Establish the Problem

Plan the smallest investigation that can answer the material questions. Before deepening a new concern, identify which required conclusion, obligation, acceptance condition, or major risk it can change; otherwise keep it local, defer it with a real trigger, or omit it. Trace a representative end-to-end scenario when an existing path matters: initiator, participants, original facts, business decisions, state changes, side effects, outcome handoff, and applicable failure response. Locate the responsible boundary for every material fact, rule, decision, state change, effect, and recovery action. Directories, interfaces, dependencies, reuse, and call order are evidence of collaboration, not proof of ownership.

For material information and handoffs, establish why the system depends on them, which source determines their meaning, what the receiver can observe, how validity and conflicts are handled, and what follows partial or indeterminate completion. Include a concept or boundary only when it carries independent business meaning, technical responsibility, decision authority, lifecycle, outcome, change boundary, trust boundary, or recovery responsibility. Preserve an unknown owner, conflicting source, invalid transition, absent recovery path, or uncontained change path as a fact instead of inventing structure to complete the model.

When change containment affects a boundary or abstraction, trace one business-rule change and one technology or integration change through their owners, contracts, dependent mechanisms, deployment, and tests. Add another scenario or source only when it can expose a materially different result, owner, branch, coupling path, failure, trust boundary, deployment condition, or viable direction.

Understand the existing system only to the depth required by the judgment. If the task requires a new or changed result, establish the design problem: the result to create, the obligations and forces that must hold together, relevant starting conditions, and the causal gap or structural tension that shapes the solution space. Greenfield work needs no existing failure; its problem is the responsibility, information, interaction, and operating model required by the new result and constraints. Proceed when the relevant operation is evidence-bounded, the problem is explicit, and all route-changing conditions are visible. Stop when more evidence cannot change the judgment.

## Stage 3: Form the System Judgment

Build the answer around the required result, using only the facts, constraints, and causal relationships that can change it. Existing operation may supply the explanation, a starting condition, or an evolution constraint; it is not a mandatory first step. When no design choice is required, synthesize the relevant outcome chain, responsibilities or gaps, inputs and results, information and state, handoffs, failure behavior, and evidence boundary, then stop. Do not turn an observed gap into an unsolicited target.

When the result requires design, form genuinely viable directions around the problem before committing to a route. Consider a local or direct change, reuse of an existing responsible boundary or maintained capability, deletion of unnecessary machinery, reassignment of responsibility or information authority, and a structurally different direction when each is plausible. Include the current route only when it remains viable. Do not manufacture a fixed number of alternatives or distinguish implementations that are interchangeable at overview level.

Close each plausible direction only far enough to compare its observable result, responsibilities, material contracts, necessary information and state, handoff and failure semantics, change propagation, operating burden, evolution, reversibility, and evidence. Eliminate invalid directions by obligations and constraints, then use the design standard and hierarchical selection among the remainder.

For a consequential choice that changes the overall route, a key responsibility boundary, an external contract, or material lifecycle cost, retain a compact selection proof: the selected direction, obligations it satisfies, strongest viable alternative, why a simpler route is insufficient, cost bearer, evidence boundary, and reconsideration condition. Do not create a decision record for a constraint, fact, inevitable consequence, or interchangeable implementation. Keep a route conditional when missing evidence or external authority can change it.

Select the best-supported smallest complete direction and form its operating model from the result and constraints: initiation, collaboration, facts, authorized decisions, necessary state, effects, handoffs, observable result, and failure takeover. Determine which affected responsibilities and paths remain, move, merge, or disappear. Shape boundaries and dependencies by responsibility and change containment—not the package tree, a desire for more layers, or a desire for fewer components.

## Stage 4: Close, Challenge, and Converge the Judgment

**Establish completeness.** Use the Overall Design for the global operating model, cross-mechanism constraints, dependency direction, and handoffs. Use a Key Design only for an independent mechanism necessary to explain or produce a material outcome. It must have a clear input, result, responsibility, and operating boundary; a proposed Key Design must also own a necessary outcome, correctness condition, or material constraint that would otherwise be unsupported. A repository, service, table, interface, state store, or multiple consumers neither qualifies nor disqualifies it.

Map every conclusion to its basis:

- Map explained behavior to the evidence, environment, and responsible mechanism or gap that establishes it.
- Map every outcome, preserved behavior, and constraint to an owner, mechanism, and observable result.
- Map every proposed boundary, dependency, abstraction, state, representation, and compatibility path to an established obligation, change-containment need, active evolution need, or named risk.

Remove anything without such a basis. When changing an existing system, derive the affected surface from semantics rather than a file list. Cover applicable facts and triggers, contracts and consumers, information and state, effects, trust and permissions, failure and recovery, observability, deployment, migration, rollback, and acceptance.

**Challenge decisive scenarios.** Use the few end-to-end scenarios most likely to disprove or materially change the judgment. Simulate actual facts, decisions, information changes, state transitions, responsibility handoffs, effects, and consumer-visible results across applicable success, failure, partial completion, concurrency, degradation, branches, re-entry, migration, rollback, and change propagation. Inspect only scenarios that can change a material conclusion. Bound unsupported behavior as unknown instead of completing it by inference.

A crash window does not prove that a proposed recovery guarantee is required. First ask whether removing the mechanism or guarantee preserves every established obligation. Once durable business state or a committed effect is established, fully close its real lifecycle and recovery. Support an external guarantee with evidence from the owner rather than an interface declaration, SDK behavior, or mock result alone. Model coexistence, compatibility, cutover, rollback, irreversible points, and old-path exit only when an established change actually requires them.

**Converge and reconcile.** Reopen only conclusions affected by a disproved premise, new fact, changed constraint, failed scenario, or stronger viable alternative. Resolve judgment-changing uncertainty, make the affected conclusion conditional, or suspend it. Restate the required judgment and selected route; if a secondary concern or speculative future now dominates the work without changing an obligation, route, acceptance condition, or major risk, remove the excess and recenter. Converge when further evidence or viable alternatives cannot materially change what the reader must understand or decide—not when every implementation detail is known.

For completion or rewrite, reconcile the formed judgment with the revision anchor and existing artifact. Account for every anchored outcome, material fact, preserved behavior, non-goal, constraint, adopted route, and acceptance meaning as preserved, corrected by evidence, changed by an authorized source, or no longer applicable for a stated reason. Confirm that a correction did not silently change the goal, add a guarantee, redesign an adopted route, or shift the document's focus.

Use a working change plan only when several interdependent revisions or omission risks exist. Derive it from semantic differences against the anchor: what must be preserved, corrected, changed, or removed; its direct dependent surfaces; and the evidence that closes it. The plan controls revision completeness and is not the revision record. A design revision exists when an Overall or Key Design mechanism, responsibility boundary, or consequential design choice is added, removed, replaced, reassigned, or materially changed, including because of new evidence. Evidence collection, review rounds, wording, structure, and formatting are not design revisions when the design is unchanged.

Use design status only when the artifact proposes a design: `Drafting` while the route or key mechanisms remain unformed, `Pending Confirmation` when the complete route has not been adopted, and `Confirmed` only when the user or authorized owner adopts the latest route and consequential choices. Earlier confirmation does not survive an intent or route change. Status does not prove implementation.

## Stage 5: Draft and Reconcile the Artifact

After the judgment converges, read `references/overview-standard.md` in full and follow its language, structure, and reconciliation rules. Draft only the content required to express the judgment. Keep facts within their evidence and make proposed design and implementation status unambiguous where confusion is possible. Omit investigation steps, abandoned routes, review history, approval ceremony, feedback-by-feedback responses, and method commentary unless a rejected route still determines a live tradeoff or evolution condition.

For completion or rewrite, compare the finished document with the revision anchor, still-material existing content, and the working change plan when used. Confirm that it expresses the latest applicable judgment rather than an intermediate draft; every preserved or reassigned responsibility has an owner; every changed or removed mechanism has a basis and closed downstream consequences; and restructuring did not drop a valid mechanism, handoff, failure response, or acceptance obligation. If semantic reconciliation fails, return to the stage that owns the gap instead of concealing it with polish.
