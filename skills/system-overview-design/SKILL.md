# System Overview Design

Develop one system-level judgment through five stages: frame intent, goals, and scope; build an evidence-backed system model; synthesize the current state or form and select a target design; validate and converge; then draft and reconcile the document. The stages govern how the judgment develops. The system-design standard governs every stage rather than adding another stage, and `references/overview-standard.md` governs only how completed judgments are expressed.

Use one continuous reasoning chain. Establish the judgment the document must support, the observable outcome and preserved behavior, the constraints that must hold, and the evidence available. Reconstruct how the system produces the outcome through participants, facts, decisions, state, handoffs, side effects, and failure recovery. Close responsibility, state, boundary contracts, dependency direction, and recovery before selecting technical mechanisms. In target-design mode, derive and challenge the smallest complete design that can satisfy those obligations; in current-state mode, stop at the strongest explanation the evidence supports. Try to disprove the result through representative operating and change scenarios, reopen only dependent conclusions, and draft only after the design converges.

Use the real outcome and who guarantees it as the organizing thread. The overall design owns the global outcome chain, cross-mechanism constraints, and handoffs. Key designs own independent mechanisms that produce necessary outcomes and the choices that shape them. Evolution owns coexistence, cutover, rollback, and exit. Evidence limits the strength of every premise and conclusion. The document describes the current explanation or selected design; it does not replace an accountable owner's adoption decision or proof of implementation.

## System Design Standard

Apply the following properties as causal tests, not document sections, slogans, or a scorecard. First establish the non-negotiable obligations: observable goals, preserved behavior, external contracts, authorized degradation, correctness conditions, and material constraints. Reject a target direction that violates them. Compare tradeoffs only among valid directions, and change an obligation only through a traceable user or accountable-owner decision.

For a current-state overview, report evidenced behavior and gaps only when they affect the in-scope explanation; do not force the current system to satisfy a target standard or turn the overview into an unsolicited audit. For a target design, satisfy every applicable property without adding structure merely to demonstrate compliance.

- **Outcome correctness:** Make every mechanism serve a design obligation or bound a named risk. Verify the stable result an actual consumer or responsible party can rely on through material success, failure, concurrency, and degradation. Do not substitute local technical progress for the business or operational outcome.
- **Ownership and decisions:** Locate one primary boundary responsible for the meaning and correctness of each material fact, rule, decision, state change, side effect, and failure response, or preserve the evidenced gap. Distinguish an executor or recovery owner only when crossing the boundary changes an outcome, correctness condition, contract, or failure behavior. Bind such handoffs through contract without creating competing decision sources, duplicated policy, or ownerless results and failures.
- **State source and lifecycle:** Retain state only when a decision, correctness condition, observable outcome, handoff, recovery path, or operating responsibility depends on it. Identify the source or conflict rule that determines the result, who may write, and the applicable creation, transition, validity, invalidation, reconciliation, and recovery rules. Keep events, caches, projections, and copies subordinate unless an explicit contract gives them authoritative meaning. Reject state or representation that closes no named responsibility, correctness, operating, or recovery gap.
- **Boundaries and change containment:** Form boundaries from responsibility, change drivers, contracts, trust, lifecycle, and failure scope rather than the package tree. Make material contracts and dependency direction explicit, hide internal technology and storage choices, and keep business policy independently changeable from transport, persistence, framework, vendor, deployment, and presentation when their owners or change drivers differ. Localize necessary coupling instead of maximizing separation; shared data, cycles, synchronous availability, and coordinated releases require a correctness or operating basis.
- **Justified structure:** Add a boundary, abstraction, state, protocol, cache, extension point, or compatibility path only when the simplest existing boundary leaves a demonstrated responsibility, correctness, change-containment, operating, or evolution gap. Weigh the gap closed against indirection, synchronization, operation, migration, and removal cost. Remove structure that changes no required outcome, ownership boundary, correctness condition, contained change, or material risk.
- **Runtime and evolution closure:** Carry each material outcome through handoffs, partial or indeterminate completion, failure detection, recovery, observability, and evolution. Make the recovery responsibility explicit when an outcome and its record or handoff cannot be completed atomically. Trace semantic changes through affected facts, decisions, contracts, consumers, state and data, trust, side effects, operations, migration, rollback, and acceptance, inspecting only dimensions that can change a material conclusion.

## Frame Intent, Goals, and Scope

Choose current-state overview or target design as the content mode, and create, complete, or rewrite as the editing mode. Define the understanding or direction judgment the document must support, the observable operation or result in scope, the behavior to preserve, and the participants, responsibilities, flows, contracts, environments, and time horizon that matter. Revise the frame when later evidence changes a premise. Treat the current system as evidence and an evolution constraint in target-design mode, not as the automatic target.

For a target design, separate the observable goal, symptom, causal diagnosis, preserved behavior, allowed degradation, external constraints, inherited decisions, and candidate means. Turn capacity, latency, security, privacy, compliance, reliability, and operability into observable constraints only when they can change the outcome, responsibility boundary, route, failure behavior, evolution, or acceptance. Derive acceptable complexity from risk, reversibility, evidenced volatility, and operating context rather than speculative reuse. Test any causal premise that can change scope, guarantee strength, acceptance, or direction. When the current mechanism already meets the goal or a local change is sufficient, state the basis and stop expanding the system-level design.

For a completion or rewrite, identify existing goals, responsibilities, contracts, mechanisms, decisions, failure and evolution behavior that still matter, together with their sources, dependencies, and editable scope. Preserve their meaning until the system model determines what remains, moves, changes, or disappears. Preserve an adopted overall route unless a locatable user or accountable-owner decision changes it or the user authorizes redesign against goals and constraints. Keep a newly selected route pending confirmation. Stop affected work when a missing owner decision controls the route, and stop polishing an unsound route unless redesign is authorized.

Match sources to claims: user outcomes and accountable-owner decisions establish intent, contracts establish external commitments, runtime evidence establishes behavior in a specific environment, implementation establishes the current path, and tests establish protected expectations. Record material conflicts and applicability. Distinguish facts, constraints, inherited decisions, new choices, and consequences. Keep an assumption as an unverified premise, a risk as an adverse outcome possible under the intended design, and an unresolved condition as missing evidence or authority that prevents or qualifies a conclusion. A document label such as “confirmed” proves only that the content was stated.

Treat a decision as inherited only when a locatable user or authorized-owner decision confirms it. Preserve behavior only when an active contract, real consumer, production dependency, or accountable owner requires it; implementation and tests prove existence, not indefinite compatibility. Realize a still-valid inherited decision without manufacturing another option contest. When evidence invalidates it, name the original decision, change basis, and affected flows, contracts, designs, and evolution.

Do not determine final design status during framing. Support implementation status only with implementation or runtime evidence. Use design versions only when the project already has that convention, and omit feedback rounds from the finished document. Move forward once the judgment, goals, scope, modes, preservation responsibilities, and route-changing unresolved conditions can be stated independently.

## Build the Evidence-Backed System Model

Plan the smallest investigation that can answer the overview's material questions. Select implementation, test, contract, or runtime evidence according to impact, failure consequence, uncertainty, reversibility, and cross-boundary cost. Treat directories, modules, interfaces, and dependencies as leads to actual responsibility and operation. Stop when more evidence can no longer change the factual boundary, responsibility, contract, route, or material design choice.

Trace one representative end-to-end scenario across the initiator, participants, original facts, business decisions, state changes, side effects, outcome handoff, and failure recovery. Locate the responsible boundary for each material fact, rule, decision, state change, side effect, and failure response. Trace a distinct executor, representation maintainer, or recovery owner only when the handoff can change the result, applicable correctness rule, contract, conflict rule, or failure behavior. For material state and handoffs, identify why the system depends on them, which source determines their meaning, how validity and conflicts are handled, what contract applies, and what the receiving party can observe after partial or indeterminate completion.

When the judgment depends on a boundary, abstraction, or expected change, trace one representative business-rule change and one technology or integration change through their owners, contracts, dependent mechanisms, data, deployment, and tests. Add another relationship, scenario, or state detail only when it can expose a materially different owner, result, branch, coupling path, failure, trust boundary, deployment condition, or evolution constraint. Reconstruct current operation before designing change; do not infer the target from the module layout or input document structure.

Include a concept or boundary only when it carries independent business meaning, technical responsibility, decision authority, lifecycle, system outcome, change boundary, or failure recovery. Distinguish domain rules that must remain, implementation that may change, and incidental structure that should disappear from a target design. Treat reuse, dependency order, and call order as evidence of collaboration, not proof of ownership. Preserve a missing owner, conflicting source, invalid or unknown transition, absent recovery path, or uncontained change path as a current fact or responsibility gap instead of inventing a boundary to complete the model.

Separate unresolved conditions that can change the route from those that only qualify one responsibility or can proceed under an explicit condition. A current-state overview may proceed when the model explains the in-scope participants, responsibilities, outcome chain, material state, side effects, handoffs, failures, constraints, conflicts, and evidence gaps. A target design may proceed only when route-changing conditions are resolved or made explicit.

## Synthesize the Current State or Form and Select a Target Design

### Synthesize the Current State

Synthesize only verified responsibilities and operation. Use the overall design to explain the global outcome chain and representative flow, and use key designs to explain the independent mechanisms necessary to understand current operation. For each mechanism, state its role, owner or responsibility gap, inputs and outputs, state, result, failure behavior, and evidence scope. Explain a historical choice only when traceable evidence exists. Do not invent a rationale, alternative, inherited decision, improvement, or acceptance claim.

### Form and Select the Target Design

Derive the required operating model from the observable goal, preserved behavior, and constraints: initiation, collaboration, facts, authorized decisions, material state, side effects, handoffs, observable results, and failure takeover. Close responsibility, state, contract, handoff, and failure semantics before treating a technical mechanism or deployment shape as selected. Then determine which current responsibilities and paths remain, merge, move, or disappear. Do not assemble the design from modules or fill unspecified failure, rollback, compatibility, or operating behavior with “keep the existing behavior.”

Widen before committing at each decision that can materially change the route or a key mechanism. Consider reuse or direct change to the existing boundary and any genuinely viable, structurally different direction; do not manufacture a fixed number of alternatives. Deepen only plausible directions until their outcomes, responsibilities, contracts, state and handoff semantics, failure behavior, change propagation, operating cost, evolution, reversibility, and risk can be compared. Eliminate directions that cannot satisfy the design obligations or obtain sufficient evidence.

Before selecting a stateful, cross-boundary, or otherwise non-obvious mechanism, state the required behavior, the current or simplest viable means, its evidenced gap, and how the proposal closes that gap. Require stronger evidence as a choice adds persistence, synchronization, operating ownership, migration, or irreversibility. Keep a route-changing choice unresolved when evidence is insufficient; specify only behavior and contract when implementations are interchangeable at overview level. Challenge every larger direction with the strongest viable existing or simpler alternative, and select the smallest complete design that satisfies the system-design standard.

Form modules, boundaries, abstractions, state, and dependencies by applying the system-design standard. When plausible within the stated horizon and material to a boundary or abstraction judgment, verify that the applicable business-rule and technology or integration changes remain within their intended owners and contracts. Do not split responsibilities merely to create layers, combine independent responsibilities merely to reduce component count, duplicate business policy in adapters, or hide domain-specific behavior behind a generic platform abstraction that erases its owner and failure behavior.

### Close the Design Structure

Identify key designs from the outcome chain. In a target design, an independent design unit must own a necessary outcome, correctness condition, or material constraint, have a clear owner and explainable operating and failure boundary, and leave that outcome, condition, or constraint unsupported if removed. In a current-state overview, include only an evidenced mechanism necessary to understand how the current result is produced and having an explainable input, result, and operating boundary. A missing owner, recovery path, conflict rule, or evidence source belongs with the mechanism it affects or in the overall evidence gaps; a gap alone is not a key design. A service, repository, state store, or multiple consumers neither qualifies nor disqualifies a key design. Modules, fields, interfaces, transactions, notifications, storage choices, and ordinary errors are not key designs unless their boundary changes system responsibility, outcome, or failure behavior.

Assign shared content first to the mechanism that owns its facts, decisions, or state. Return a rule to that mechanism when it cannot independently own an outcome or correctness condition. Put in the overall design only rules that govern how multiple mechanisms jointly produce an outcome, hand off responsibility, or handle cross-boundary failure. Put in evolution only coexistence, cutover, rollback, and exit. Treat a new choice as real only when a genuine alternative changes responsibility, contract, outcome, correctness, failure, cost, or evolution; do not package a constraint, current fact, inevitable consequence, or interchangeable implementation as a choice. Keep the selection, basis, cost bearer, and reconsideration condition with the mechanism it shapes. A stable result does not require a new completion state, version protocol, task, event, or record; add one only when the existing boundary cannot express the result, validity, or recovery responsibility.

Close the design through four mappings:

- Map every goal, preserved behavior, and material constraint to a responsible boundary, key design, and observable result; in current-state mode, map every material operating conclusion to a mechanism, responsibility or gap, and evidence.
- Map each material responsibility and state to the fact, decision, correctness rule, result, handoff, recovery path, or operating need that justifies it, together with the applicable source, lifecycle, contract, and recovery owner.
- Map each new boundary, dependency, mechanism, abstraction, state, and compatibility path back to a demonstrated responsibility, correctness, change-containment, operating, evolution, or risk gap.
- Derive the affected surface from semantic changes rather than the proposed file list, tracing applicable upstream facts and triggers, downstream contracts and consumers, state and data, side effects, trust and permissions, failure and recovery, observability, deployment, migration, rollback, and acceptance. Record non-obvious exclusions in the working analysis and only material consequences in the finished document.

Structural closure proves completeness of the model, not behavioral reachability. For a completion or rewrite, compare the formed design with the existing one and decide what to preserve, reassign, revise, or remove. Record a design revision only when an overall or key-design mechanism, or a decision that shapes it, is added, removed, replaced, reassigned, or materially changed. Review activity, feedback events, evidence collection, wording, structure, and formatting do not constitute design revisions.

When multiple interdependent revisions or omission risks exist, make a working change plan before drafting. Order the changes, assign each to the overall design, a key design, evolution, or acceptance, and trace affected flows and validation. Apply a single independently closable change directly. Move to validation only after every material existing goal, responsibility, contract, mechanism, state obligation, and decision has an explicit outcome.

## Validate and Converge

For a current-state overview, verify that every claim matches current evidence: actual participants, state, and handoffs can restate the representative flow; conflicts bound applicability; prose does not fill unresolved conditions or responsibility gaps; and the covered system and time range are clear. Do not use target choices or acceptance criteria to prove the current system retroactively.

For a target design, turn the design obligations, material risks, and applicable quality properties into validation criteria. Select the few success, failure, partial-completion, concurrency, branch, parallel, re-entry, degradation, migration, and representative change scenarios most likely to disprove or change the design. Simulate actual state transitions and responsibility handoffs to determine whether the consumer's result remains reachable, correctness conditions hold, conflicts converge, failure responsibility stays explicit, and required constraints hold. Verify that prose, state descriptions, diagrams, and acceptance describe the same actual branch, including parallel and re-entry paths. Expand only paths where a user, consumer, operator, owner, or dependent system can observe a different result or change burden.

When an outcome cannot be produced atomically with its record or handoff, determine from traceable facts whether it occurred, did not occur, or remains indeterminate, and identify who recovers. When an authoritative source and representation can diverge, verify conflict detection, arbitration, and reconciliation without granting accidental decision power to the representation. Do not present automatic retry as safe recovery without contract evidence.

For original facts that may change or expire after a decision, trace every dependent decision, authorization, route, and presentation. State which conclusions remain valid, become invalid, or must be recomputed, and who decides again. For a derived result on which the design depends, verify the source, freshness boundary, transformation rule, unavailable semantics, and recovery path. Obtain evidence proportionate to any external guarantee; an interface declaration, SDK behavior, or mock result is not a platform guarantee.

When changing an existing system, treat every evolution stage as a real operating state. Verify old and new responsibilities, read and write relationships, compatibility and cutover boundaries, irreversible points, rollback locations, and exit conditions for the old path. Identify the first stage that satisfies the target; infrastructure readiness is not target completion.

If a scenario, change-propagation trace, simpler alternative, or new fact changes a premise, reopen only dependent overall and key designs, choices, failure semantics, validation, evolution, and revisions. Resolve a condition that can change the route, system responsibility, external contract, or user outcome; make the design explicitly conditional; or suspend the affected judgment. Keep a local unresolved condition with its owning design and state its impact and smallest confirmation method. Converge when no remaining evidence or viable alternative can materially change the route, responsibilities, dependency direction, outcome semantics, justified complexity, affected surface, satisfaction of constraints, or major risks—not when every implementation detail is known.

Determine final status only after convergence. Do not assign a design status to a current-state overview; it reaches the drafting threshold when current evidence supports the in-scope explanation. For a target design, use “Drafting” while the route or key mechanisms remain unformed, “Pending Confirmation” when the complete current design has not been explicitly adopted, and “Confirmed” only when the user or authorized owner adopts the current overall route and the key mechanisms or choices that determine it. Record the basis for confirmation. An earlier acceptance, permission to retain a route, or requested revision does not confirm the revised design, and design status does not prove implementation.

## Draft and Reconcile the Design Document

After completing the design judgments, read `references/overview-standard.md` in full and use its mode, section, language, and reconciliation rules to organize them. Its drafting checks may expose a missing judgment but must not supply one; return the gap to the stage that owns it. Do not turn the investigation, candidate search, or design method into the document structure.

Present only the current explanation or selected target design. Record substantive design changes according to the reference, and omit the record when none exist. Exclude review and approval history, feedback history, comment-by-comment responses, rejected proposals, and abandoned candidates unless they still determine a current mechanism or evolution path.

For a completion or rewrite, compare the finished document with the existing design content that still matters and, for a complex revision, the working change plan. Confirm that preserved or reassigned content still has an owning section, changed or removed content has its basis and downstream effects closed, and deleted or merged sections did not remove a valid mechanism, handoff, failure, or acceptance responsibility.

Apply the drafting checks in the reference. If the document does not survive semantic reconciliation, return to the relevant design stage instead of concealing the gap with polish.
